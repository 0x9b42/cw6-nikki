[340] In the same way as he divides himself into bull and cow, Prajapati also divides himself into the two principles manas (mind) and vac (speech):

This world was Prajapati alone, vac was his self, and vac his second self. He meditated: This vac I will send forth, and she shall go hence and pervade all things. Then he sent forth vac , and she went and filled the universe.

This passage is of especial interest in that speech is conceived as a creative, extraverted movement of libido, a diastole in Goethe’s sense. There is a further parallel in the following passage:

In truth Prajapati was this world, and with him was vac his second self. He copulated with her; she conceived; she went forth out of him, and made these creatures, and once again entered into Prajapati.
