[286] This scene is a perfect illustration of Jordan’s words: “Society must be pleased if possible; if it will not be pleased, it must be astonished; if it will neither be pleased nor astonished, it must be pestered and shocked.” In the East a rich man proclaims his rank by never showing himself in public unless supported by two slaves. Epimetheus affects this pose in order to make an impression. Well-doing must at the same time be combined with admonition and moral instruction. And, as that does not produce an effect, the other must at least be horrified by the picture of his own baseness. Everything is aimed at creating an impression. There is an American saying that runs: “In America two kinds of men make good—the man who can do , and the man who can bluff.” Which means that pretence is sometimes just as successful as actual performance. An extravert of this kind prefers to work by appearance . The introvert tries to do it by force and misuses his work to that end.
